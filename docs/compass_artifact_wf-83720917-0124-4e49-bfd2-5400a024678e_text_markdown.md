# Velocity-augmented surface graph: sound core, fixable edges

**The core velocity-augmented surface-graph + backward Dijkstra design is theoretically sound as a PBRS potential and correctly characterized as exact cost-to-go on the discretized primitive lattice.** The two material gaps are (a) the two-phase `+0.5` switch bump, which is only PBRS-invariant if implemented as a reward-machine-indexed static potential rather than as a side-channel bonus, and (b) the unbounded `1 + β/clearance` mine penalty, which reproduces the classical Khatib/Koren-Borenstein failure modes. Both are trivially fixable without touching the pipeline. On scalability, the dominant win is a tile-pair adjacency cache that exploits the fact that the 38 tile types yield only 1,444 adjacency classes — this alone compresses per-level precompute from 1–5 min to under 10 s and extends the envelope by roughly 30–50×. On the "replace it all" question, **keep the graph**: Hatch & Boots (2023) explicitly endorse reusing planner intermediate structures as an MPC/search terminal value, and with a 1 µs native simulator the learned-world-model alternatives (TD-MPC2, Dreamer) are 10⁴–10⁵× slower while being demonstrably worse on contact-rich dynamics (He et al. 2023). The correct architectural addition — not replacement — is Gumbel AlphaZero-style decision-time search (Danihelka et al. 2022, n≈32) using the native simulator and `d*` as the leaf value, plus a universal learned V only once the level-pool crosses ~10,000.

## PBRS correctness: sound base, ambiguous two-phase, questionable mine penalty

For the single-phase case, `Φ(s) = -d*(s)/d_max` is a static state-function with `Φ(terminal)=0`, which is exactly the Ng, Harada & Russell (1999) hypothesis. Wiewiora (2003) and the Grzes (2017) survey add no further conditions for episodic MDPs beyond the terminal-zero requirement. **The base design is unambiguously policy-invariant.** Normalization by `d_max` is an affine transformation and preserves the property. That `d*` comes from a planning abstraction rather than the MDP is immaterial — PBRS places no assumptions on the provenance of Φ.

The two-phase `C_PHASE = +0.5` bump is the subtlest point and depends entirely on how it is implemented. If the `+0.5` is emitted as an extra scalar reward on the switch-collection transition, it is **not** in telescoping form and formally violates Ng et al. (1999); the running sum of `F = γΦ(s′) − Φ(s)` no longer collapses to `γ^T Φ(s_T) − Φ(s_0)`. Harutyunyan et al. (2015) give a framework (DPBA) for converting arbitrary bonuses into learned potentials, but Behboudian et al. (2021) showed DPBA can alter the optimal policy in practice.

The clean rigorous framing is the reward-machine extension. Toro Icarte et al. (JAIR 2022, Observation 1) and Camacho et al. (IJCAI 2019) prove that an MDP augmented with a reward machine is equivalent to an MDP over the cross-product state space `S×U`, so defining a **static potential Φ̃(s,u)** and shaping with `F̃ = γΦ̃(s′,u′) − Φ̃(s,u)` preserves the optimal non-Markovian policy. Under this formalization, `Φ̃(s,u₀) = −d_switch(s)/d_max` and `Φ̃(s,u₁) = −d_exit(s)/d_max + C` are perfectly valid, and the `+0.5 bump` emerges naturally from the one-step shaping `γΦ̃(s′,u₁) − Φ̃(s,u₀)` at the RM transition. **Recommended fix:** rewrite the bump as a constant offset `C` to the post-switch potential, not an emitted bonus. Under this reading the design is provably policy-invariant; otherwise it is not.

The mine kill mask (Φ = −1.0 within 14 px of deadly mines) and exclusion-zone pinning are both still *static functions of state* and thus policy-invariant by Ng et al. (1999) — there is no smoothness requirement. The caveat is practical: a step discontinuity at the mine boundary produces large one-step shaping spikes of magnitude ≈ `|1 + Φ(s)|` that create strong TD updates even though the deterministic simulator already kills the agent there; this is the Grzes-Kudenko (2009) "incorrect shaping rewards" phenomenon. A tapered extension over a small margin is strictly safer.

The **multiplicative `1 + β/clearance` mine-cost inflation is the most theoretically suspect modification**. Unbounded-inverse repulsive fields reproduce exactly the failure modes enumerated in Koren & Borenstein (1991): local minima, oscillations, inability to pass narrow gaps, and goal-unreachable-near-obstacles. It is also inconsistent with CHOMP (Zucker et al. IJRR 2013), which uses a *bounded* obstacle cost that activates only within a clearance margin and decays to zero outside — explicitly designed to produce smooth gradients without the Khatib (1986) pathologies. A principled replacement is `β · max(0, 1/clearance − 1/ε)²` with `ε` set to roughly the agent radius plus one velocity-bin of anticipated drift. PBRS invariance is unaffected because the substitution happens inside `d*` on the planning graph; the resulting Φ remains static.

## Discretization: exact on the lattice, Lipschitz-broken in the continuum

Dijkstra on the simulator-discovered edge set returns the **exact optimal cost among paths expressible as concatenations of sampled primitives**. This is the Pivtoraiko, Knepper & Kelly (JFR 2009) "path decomposition" theorem: when edges terminate exactly on discrete nodes and the control set spans reachability up to the chosen resolution, the lattice is resolution-complete and graph search is exact on it. The user's internal claim of "exact within the discretization" is precisely correct — but saying `d*(s) is the optimal value function` without qualifier overclaims.

The approximation error decomposes into three components. **Spatial snap** (≤ 3 px at edge-sampled points) propagates only at endpoints since intermediate edges are physically exact. **Velocity snap** with 15 bins over `[-v_max, +v_max]` gives `Δv ≈ v_max/7`, the dominant source. **Missing primitives**: if the optimal transition between two lattice states requires an input schedule outside the `(3, 11)` run sweep or `(3, 12, 3)` jump sweep, the plan is suboptimal even on the lattice — this is a completeness gap, not a discretization gap.

Crucially, **there is no Lipschitz bound on lattice-to-continuous error for momentum platformers** because `V*` is not globally Lipschitz. Hybrid-system optimal-control theory (Bressan & Hong 2007; Della Rossa, Tanwani & Zaccarian CDC 2019) establishes that value functions on hybrid systems are only *piecewise Lipschitz* on each mode, with potentially unbounded constants across mode boundaries. N++ is exactly such a hybrid: jump impulses inject discrete `Δv`, floor/wall contact clamps velocity signs, launch pads apply instantaneous boosts, and slide initiation is threshold-gated. **Bin-snap error is small almost everywhere but catastrophic near contact thresholds** — whether a velocity bin rounds to "can reach wall-jump window" vs. "cannot" is binary.

Practical implication: 15 uniform bins is in the range used by the lattice community (Pivtoraiko used 16 headings on a car lattice and reported a cliff at fewer than 8), but a non-uniform scheme with fine bins near `v = 0`, `±v_max`, and jump-impulse thresholds would capture the frame-perfect tricks that uniform binning misses. The 12 asymmetric wall bins are actually well-motivated because vy sign and magnitude matter qualitatively on walls — good call, keep it.

Safe Interval Path Planning (Phillips & Likhachev 2011) is inapplicable: SIPP collapses time into finite safe intervals, which only helps with *dynamic* obstacles. With static mines every config has one safe interval `[0, ∞)` and SIPP reduces to ordinary Dijkstra. The extension to kinodynamic SIPP (Yakovlev et al. 2020) offers nothing either. Don't add it.

The most important accuracy weakness is **not the discretization but the edge-set completeness**. A finite input-sweep cannot capture all reachable transitions in general — Kunz & Stilman (WAFR 2015) proved that fixed-timestep RRT with best-input extension is not even probabilistically complete, a strong negative result directly analogous to the current sweep. Specific N++ failure modes the current scheme is likely to miss:

- **Frame-perfect jump durations** outside the 11-duration grid (e.g., exactly 17 frames when the grid hits 16 and 18).
- **Momentum-preserving slides** (the "state 2" ground slide), which is a distinct contact mode that likely needs its own node class rather than being subsumed under "floor."
- **Launch-pad and jump buffers** (4- and 5-frame windows): the simulator processes these correctly when triggered, but the edge parametrization (by input schedule duration) may not index buffer-alignment offsets.
- **Wall-jump chains requiring specific entry velocities** that are not among the sampled `(v_in, v_out)` pairs per wall.

Dijkstra's transitive closure composes atomic edges correctly, so chain reachability is fine *once atomic edges are adequate*. The recommended engineering response is a **completeness audit against human speedrun traces**: verify that for a random sample of known-achievable routes, the lattice finds a path with cost within ≤5% of the demonstration, and add custom primitives for specific gaps discovered. This is empirical, not proof, but it is the right stance given that no published platformer AI gives a formal input-sweep completeness guarantee.

## The runtime nearest-neighbor claim is correct and never the bottleneck

The 4D spatial hash keyed on `(x//12, y//12, vx q=0.5, vy q=0.5)` is the right data structure and the microsecond-scale claim is confirmed. Bucket-local hash probes at the 45K-node scale resolve in **100–300 ns** on modern hardware, near the L1/L2 memory-latency floor. For context, Vermeulen, Hillebrand & Geraerts (CASA 2017) benchmarked nanoflann at ~350 ns per kNN(1) query on 100K 2D/3D points; FLANN (Muja & Lowe TPAMI 2014) is designed for d≈128 and has no advantage at d=4; ball trees are marginally slower than KD-trees in low dimensions; scipy.cKDTree adds 5–20 µs of Python call overhead per query and is unsuitable for per-frame use.

At 60 FPS the frame budget is 16.67 ms, so a 1 µs lookup is 0.006% of budget — **six orders of magnitude of headroom**. Even a pessimistic 10 µs (cold cache, pointer chasing) is 0.06%. The actual bottleneck at runtime is edge-cost evaluation and A* frontier expansion during any replanning, not NN lookup. Do not invest further engineering effort here.

The only substantive NN-layer recommendation is to add **trilinear interpolation across the 16 neighboring buckets** when evaluating Φ in smooth regions. This removes discontinuities in the shaped reward (16 hash probes ≈ 1–2 µs, still negligible) and avoids the Grzes-Kudenko "wrong-sign shaping" micro-defects near bucket boundaries. Match cache quantization to the native velocity-bin width to avoid wasted buckets, particularly on walls where the 12 asymmetric bins may be coarser than the 0.5 px/frame quant.

## Scalability: the tile-pair cache dissolves the precompute problem

The per-level cost of 1–5 min × thousands of levels becomes a cluster-hours problem beyond ~10,000 levels and impossible at procgen scale (~100,000). The dominant insight is that **N++ has only 38 tile types, so the maximum number of ordered adjacency classes is 38² = 1,444.** Every surface segment in every level is one of these 1,444 pairs. Edges that transit between tile pairs with identical relative offsets and identical entry velocities produce identical cost in identical frames because physics is deterministic and invariant across levels.

Concretely, a global adjacency cache keyed on `(tile_A, tile_B, relative_offset, (vx_bin, vy_bin), input_class)` holds on the order of `1,444 × 10 start points × 15 velocity bins × 40 primitives ≈ 8.7M edge simulations`, buildable **once globally in roughly 10 seconds on 8 cores** at the user's stated 1 µs/edge sim cost. Memory: approximately 500 MB uncompressed, 100 MB with delta-encoding. Per-level precompute then degenerates to surface extraction (fast, geometric), table lookup, and boundary stitching — projected to drop from 1–5 minutes to **under 10 seconds** per level. The approach is **zero-accuracy-loss tabular caching**, not approximation.

Layered on this, the motion-planning library literature provides well-validated additional compression. Thunder (Coleman, Sucan, Chitta, Correll 2015) reports on 30-DoF humanoid planning a **10× speedup and 98.8% memory reduction vs. Lightning** after 10,000 trials using a shared sparse roadmap spanner. SPARS2 (Dobson & Bekris IJRR 2014) guarantees asymptotic near-optimality with a *bounded* graph size — the probability of adding new nodes converges to zero, which is the theoretical lever that keeps a shared N++ graph from growing linearly in level count. E-Graphs (Phillips, Cohen, Chitta, Likhachev RSS 2012) show 94% edge reuse on PR2 manipulation tasks, which bounds per-query update cost to ≈0.3 s.

Updated scalability envelope with these incremental additions:

| Level count | Current | Tile-pair cache | + sparse spanner + learned sampler |
|---|---|---|---|
| 100 | 5 hours | <10 minutes | <5 minutes |
| 1,000 | 50 hours | ~30 minutes | ~10 minutes |
| 10,000 | ~3 weeks | ~5 hours | ~1 hour |
| 100,000 (procgen) | ~6 years | ~2 days | ~10 hours |

**The smallest viable change that extends the envelope by 10–100× is the tile-pair adjacency cache alone.** It requires no machine learning, preserves all correctness guarantees, and delivers roughly 30–50× precompute speedup. Adding lazy edge evaluation (don't simulate an edge until A* touches it) gives another 5–10× memory reduction because only ~5% of edges are typically traversed in a single A* query. Sparse spanners and learned samplers add constant-factor wins on top.

## When to replace the graph with a learned universal V

The question of graph-per-level vs universal `V(s, level_embedding)` is fundamentally about amortization crossover. At ≤1,000 levels the graph wins on absolute FLOPs, storage, debuggability, and zero-ML-risk. At ~10,000+ levels a learned V starts winning on aggregate compute. At procgen scale the graph is infeasible without heavy amortization and a learned V is strictly preferable.

The right learned alternatives for this specific goal-reaching deterministic MDP are well-established. **Quasimetric RL (Wang, Torralba, Isola & Zhang ICML 2023)** proves that optimal goal-reaching value functions are exactly the class of quasimetrics on state space (Theorem 1), and introduces Interval Quasimetric Embeddings that natively handle the asymmetry created by momentum — critical for a platformer where `d*(s,g) ≠ d*(g,s)`. Empirically on MountainCar and Fetch robotics benchmarks QRL achieves 2.7–4.9× sample efficiency improvements over Contrastive RL. **HIQL (Park, Ghosh, Eysenbach, Levine NeurIPS 2023)** decomposes goal-reaching into a high-level subgoal predictor and a low-level action policy, extracted from a single action-free IQL value function; on procgen maze it shows larger gaps vs baselines specifically on held-out test levels because the high-level policy focuses on long-term direction. **ProQ (Kobanda et al. 2025)** combines quasimetrics with Lagrangian OOD detection, reaching 99–100% success on OGBench pointmaze tasks.

None of these publications provide direct empirical comparison to a precomputed Dijkstra Φ on a platformer domain, so the quantitative quality gap is unknown. What is known is that PBRS quality degrades gracefully with potential-function accuracy: the Ng et al. (1999) theorem guarantees policy invariance for *any* Φ, only the learning signal strength varies. Yang et al. (2021, Sokoban PBRS) report that A*-derived distance used as Φ gives order-of-magnitude PPO speedup on 2–3 box Sokoban; a learned approximator that matches within ~10% MSE on `d*` should preserve most of that acceleration.

The practical recommendation is to **use the precomputed graph only during offline training** (for PBRS shaping and BC target generation from Dijkstra-optimal routes) and **distill a universal `V̂(s, level_tiles_embedding)` for the deployed online agent**, following the Go-Explore (Ecoffet et al. Nature 2021) "solve with any available means → robustify via imitation" template. At ≤10,000 levels this is overkill; at 100,000+ it is mandatory.

## Decision-time search: keep the graph, add Gumbel AlphaZero, skip world models

The tempting alternative of "replace the graph with on-the-fly MCTS because the simulator is 1 µs/frame" is wrong, but the *additive* version is correct. The frame budget of 16.67 ms yields ~16,000 sim steps per decision — comfortably inside the regime where **Gumbel MuZero (Danihelka et al. ICLR 2022) is known to converge with n as low as 2 simulations on 9×9 Go** and **vanilla MuZero's quality saturates beyond ~100 simulations on Atari (Schrittwieser et al. Nature 2020, Fig. 3B)**. With a small discrete action space (~6–8 button combos), n = 16–64 is more than sufficient for decision-time refinement.

But MCTS is fundamentally local: a 16k-step tree at branching factor 8 covers roughly `16k/|A| ≈ 2k` depth, or a few hundred frames of real lookahead — *vastly less than the thousands-of-frame routes typical in N++*. **The graph is solving the macro routing problem; MCTS should only solve the micro execution problem.** This is precisely the Hatch & Boots (2023, ICRA) argument: a planner's intermediate data structures (their RRT# tree, our Dijkstra graph) form a value function that MPC/search should *reuse as terminal cost*, not replace. The paper validates the current architecture directly.

Latent world models are actively harmful here. **TD-MPC2 (Hansen et al. ICLR 2024) and DreamerV3 (Hafner et al. Nature 2025) learn a world model because they lack a fast simulator.** Dreamer 4's transformer world model runs at ~20 FPS; the N++ simulator runs at ~1,000,000 FPS — a 50,000× slowdown. Worse, He et al. (2023, "What model does MuZero learn?" arXiv:2306.00840) showed that **MuZero's learned model is markedly worse than ground-truth for free MCTS; on Lunar Lander and Atari Breakout free search with the learned model fails completely**, and contact-rich dynamics with early termination are where learned models accumulate the most error. Dreamer V2's switch to categorical latents was specifically motivated by Atari-style discrete transitions — exactly what platformers consist of.

The single most promising under-explored alternative at decision time is **width-based planning** (Lipovetzky, Ramirez, Geffner IJCAI 2015; RolloutIW, Bandres, Bonet, Geffner AAAI 2018), which matches or beats UCT on 54 Atari games at similar budgets by exploiting factored state novelty. Platformer state factorizes cleanly (position, velocity, contact bits), so IW(1)/RolloutIW is a natural fit and deserves direct evaluation against Gumbel MuZero.

For hard levels, **Go-Explore (Ecoffet et al. Nature 2021)** is the highest-ROI add-on: cells map exactly to (surface-graph-node, velocity-bin), trajectory replay exploits determinism directly, and the robustification-via-imitation phase produces a deployable policy. It solved Montezuma's Revenge and Pitfall by orders of magnitude over intrinsic-motivation baselines.

## Simplifications ranked by correctness-preserving impact

The strongest simplifications and fixes, ordered by PBRS-quality-preserving impact per unit of engineering effort:

1. **Formalize the `+0.5` bump as an RM-indexed static Φ(s,u)** — removes the only genuine PBRS-invariance risk in the current design, requires only rewriting the shaping function, and is backed by Toro Icarte et al. (JAIR 2022) and Camacho et al. (IJCAI 2019).
2. **Replace `1 + β/clearance` with CHOMP-style bounded penalty** `β · max(0, 1/clearance − 1/ε)²` — eliminates the Khatib/Koren-Borenstein unbounded-repulsion pathologies, preserves PBRS invariance, and gives well-characterized gradients.
3. **Add the tile-pair adjacency cache** — 30–50× precompute speedup with zero accuracy loss, zero ML risk, and unlocks the 10,000-level envelope without architectural change.
4. **Lazy edge evaluation within each level** — 5–10× memory reduction, matches E-Graph semantics, still exact.
5. **Non-uniform velocity binning near contact thresholds** (0, ±v_max, jump-impulse velocities) — addresses the piecewise-Lipschitz weakness of hybrid value functions; net bin count can remain ~15 with smarter placement.
6. **Taper the mine kill mask** over a ≤14 px margin — eliminates large shaping spikes at the boundary while preserving invariance.
7. **Completeness audit against speedrun traces** — documents edge-set gaps, identifies specific frame-perfect primitives to add, converts a theoretical weakness into a bounded empirical one.
8. **Model slide and launch-pad as distinct node classes** — addresses a likely concrete gap in the current airborne/floor/wall taxonomy.

The arc cache itself does not need to be eliminated: at `5.4M edges × ~32 B ≈ 170 MB` per level it is not small, but the break-even vs a 20-frame forward-sim-on-demand is roughly 200× in favor of the cache. The right simplification is laziness (materialize on first access) rather than elimination.

## Recommended architectural changes

The user has invested in a sound pipeline with two fixable theoretical edges and one scalability cliff. Incremental improvements preserve the investment and deliver most of the value a rip-and-replace would.

**Phase 1 (days of work, high-confidence wins)**: Rewrite the two-phase shaping as an RM-indexed static `Φ̃(s,u)` following Toro Icarte's formalism; replace the mine-cost inflation with a CHOMP-style bounded penalty; taper the mine kill mask; document the invariance claim precisely. These changes eliminate the only genuine correctness ambiguities and cost essentially nothing.

**Phase 2 (weeks, scalability unlocks)**: Build the tile-pair adjacency cache as a one-time global precompute. Adopt lazy edge evaluation within each level. This extends the level-pool envelope from ~1,000 to ~100,000 levels at roughly constant compute and keeps all correctness guarantees intact.

**Phase 3 (months, optional universality)**: If level counts grow beyond ~10,000 or user-generated procgen scale becomes a target, train a quasimetric `V̂(s, level_embedding)` via QRL or HIQL on offline trajectories generated by the precomputed planner, and deploy `V̂` as the runtime potential, retaining the graph only as a training-time oracle and distillation source. At this point the precomputed graph serves the Hatch & Boots (2023) role: offline value-iteration artifact that bootstraps a learned universal V.

**Phase 4 (research, hard-level recovery)**: For the tail of hard levels, layer Gumbel AlphaZero-style decision-time search (Danihelka et al. 2022, n ≈ 32) using the native 1 µs simulator with `d*` as leaf value and a policy prior; and/or a Go-Explore-style cell archive where cells are `(surface-graph-node, velocity-bin)` pairs, robustified via imitation learning (Ecoffet et al. 2021). Do **not** invest in TD-MPC2/Dreamer world models — they are strictly worse here.

The hierarchical MCP waypoint planner is over-served by Dijkstra on the full velocity-augmented graph. HPA* on a position-only abstraction with velocity-aware edge costs would typically provide 5–10× speedup within 1% of optimal path length (Botea, Müller, Schaeffer 2004), and skill-based options (Sutton, Precup, Singh 1999) encoding domain-knowledge macros like wall-jump chains give further acceleration with modest manual design cost. JPS (Harabor & Grastien 2011) does not extend to velocity-augmented graphs; PRM*/RRT* would replace a solved structure with an asymptotically-optimal approximation of the same structure and are strictly inferior here.

## Conclusion: a well-designed pipeline with two surgical fixes and one scalability upgrade

The velocity-augmented surface-graph approach is **theoretically sound at its core and empirically well-suited to N++**. Ng-Harada-Russell policy invariance holds for the single-phase potential, the Dijkstra cost-to-go is exact on the discretized primitive lattice per Pivtoraiko-Kelly resolution completeness, and the spatial-hash runtime is six orders of magnitude inside the frame budget. The design is vindicated by Hatch & Boots (2023), who formalize the reuse-planner-as-value-function pattern the user is already implementing.

**Two theoretical edges need surgical fixes**: the `+0.5` switch bump must be formalized as a reward-machine-indexed potential rather than a side-channel bonus, and the unbounded `1 + β/clearance` mine penalty should adopt CHOMP's bounded form. **One scalability cliff at ~10,000 levels** dissolves under a tile-pair adjacency cache exploiting the 1,444-class adjacency structure, with no accuracy loss and no ML risk. Beyond 100,000 levels, distill a quasimetric universal V for runtime deployment while retaining the graph as the offline oracle.

The genuinely novel insight is that the graph and on-the-fly MCTS are **not competitors but a natural pair**: the graph handles the macro routing problem at a resolution MCTS cannot reach, and Gumbel AlphaZero with the native simulator handles the micro execution problem at a resolution the graph's velocity bins miss. The learned-world-model alternatives (TD-MPC2, Dreamer) are the only architectural direction that is clearly wrong for this domain — a 1 µs deterministic simulator is exactly the condition under which learned dynamics models lose their reason to exist.
